As Web and Multimedia Coordinator for University Dining, I focus on improving usability, accessibility, and content clarity across a high-traffic campus website. The site serves students, faculty, and visitors who rely on quick access to menus, hours, and event information.

My work centers on translating stakeholder requirements into user-friendly layouts while maintaining consistency across pages and improving accessibility standards.

<a href="https://www.uwsp.edu/dining/university-dining-services/" target="_blank">UWSP Dining Main Page</a>


## Responsibilities

- Build, maintain, and optimize front-end website content within WordPress using HTML and CSS ensuring responsive, cross-browser, and cross-device compatibility.
- Improve site navigation, accessibility, and mobile responsiveness, enhancing usability for a diverse campus audience.
- Collaborate with marketing teams and campus partners in a team-based environment to identify improvements in UX.
- Develop and publish web pages and digital promotions while maintaining consistent UI patterns and branding standards.
- Continuously evaluate and refine interfaces to improve clarity, performance, and user experience.

## Problem

Users needed quick access to dining hours, menus, and announcements

- Information was sometimes difficult to locate on mobile

- Navigation required multiple clicks

- Content density reduced scanability

- Accessibility considerations needed improvement

The goal was to simplify navigation, improve readability, and ensure responsive behavior across devices.

## Process & Impact

To improve usability, I evaluated navigation patterns and identified friction points within the existing layout. One major issue was excessive header links, which increased cognitive load and reduced clarity.

- Reduced header links from 15 to 6, consolidating related pages into structured sections

- Collaborated with my supervisor to ensure alignment with UWSP’s branding and communication goals

- Reorganized content hierarchy to prioritize high-traffic information

- Evaluated mobile behavior and adjusted layout responsiveness

- Applied accessibility-aware practices, including improved heading structure, contrast considerations, and meaningful alt text

- Iteratively refined pages based on stakeholder feedback

## Results

- Improved scanability and reduced visual clutter

- Streamlined navigation for faster access to high-priority information

- Enhanced mobile usability across key pages

- Increased consistency across the site’s layout patterns

## Reflection

Working on a live campus site reinforced the importance of balancing stakeholder requirements with user needs. I learned that small layout and hierarchy changes can significantly impact clarity and usability, particularly for mobile users.
